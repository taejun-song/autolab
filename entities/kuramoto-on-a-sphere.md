# Kuramoto Model on a Sphere

The Kuramoto model on a sphere is the generalization of the classical Kuramoto model to $N$ identical self-propelled particles moving on the unit sphere $S^{d-1} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$, with $d \geq 3$, coupled all-to-all through a $d$-dimensional vector order parameter and exhibiting unexpectedly low-dimensional dynamics that are explained by an underlying Möbius-group action on the unit ball $B^d$ equipped with its hyperbolic metric.

## Governing equations

Let $x_i \in S^{d-1} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ for $i = 1, \dots, N$ denote the positions of $N$ particles on the unit sphere. The Kuramoto sphere model is the ODE system

$$
\dot{x}_i \;=\; A_i\, x_i \;+\; Z \;-\; \langle Z, x_i\rangle\, x_i, \qquad i = 1, \dots, N,
$$

where:

- $A_i$ is an antisymmetric $d \times d$ matrix (the $d$-dimensional analogue of the intrinsic frequency $\omega_i$ in the scalar [[kuramoto-model]]);
- $Z \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is a $d$-dimensional vector that plays the role of the complex mean-field order parameter;
- The cross term $\langle Z, x_i\rangle x_i$ is the radial projection that keeps each $x_i$ on $S^{d-1}$ — without it, $x_i$ would drift off the sphere.

In almost all applications $Z$ is itself a function of the configuration, most commonly a weighted centroid $Z = \sum_i a_i x_i$ with real coefficients $a_i$. The matrices $A_i$ may depend on the configuration as well but are typically taken to be constant. The state space is the $N$-fold product $X = (S^{d-1})^N$ of dimension $N(d - 1)$.

For $d = 2$ this system reduces to the classical [[kuramoto-model]] on $S^1$ via $x_i = e^{i\theta_i}$, with $A_i$ becoming the $2 \times 2$ rotation matrix at frequency $\omega_i$. For $d = 3$ the particles live on the ordinary sphere $S^2$ — the setting most intuitively pictured as a swarm of drones or self-propelled particles in 3D space, and the easiest case to visualize.

## Proposed applications

The model has been proposed in the literature to describe collective dynamics of swarms, drones, or other self-propelled entities moving in $d$-dimensional space (see [[lipton-mirollo-strogatz-2021-kuramoto-on-sphere]] §I). The sphere constraint $|x_i| = 1$ models either unit-vector orientations (heading vectors of drones) or position on a spherical manifold (particles confined to a planet surface). The "Kuramoto" structure of the coupling term $Z - \langle Z, x_i\rangle x_i$ is the simplest all-to-all coupling that preserves the sphere constraint and reduces to ordinary Kuramoto in $d = 2$.

## Unexpectedly low-dimensional dynamics

Numerical simulations of the model for $d \geq 3$ revealed a surprise: despite having $N(d-1)$ state-space dimensions, the dynamics exhibit **low-dimensional behaviour** with many constants of motion. Specifically, trajectories are confined to invariant manifolds of dimension $d(d+1)/2$ for all $N \geq 3$, independent of $N$. This is much smaller than the full state-space dimension $N(d-1)$ — for $d = 3$ and $N = 100$, the dynamics live on a 6-dimensional manifold inside a 200-dimensional state space.

Before [[lipton-mirollo-strogatz-2021-kuramoto-on-sphere]], this observation was empirical and lacked a structural explanation.

## Group-theoretic explanation

Lipton, Mirollo, and Strogatz trace the low-dimensional behaviour to a **Möbius-group action on $B^d$**. Concretely:

1. **The Möbius group** $G$ of orientation-preserving isometries of the hyperbolic metric $ds = 2|dx|/(1 - |x|^2)$ on $B^d$ is a Lie group of dimension $d(d+1)/2$, generated by boost transformations $M_w$ (parameterised by $w \in B^d$) and rotations $\zeta \in SO(d)$.
2. **The sphere-Kuramoto vector field is an infinitesimal generator of this group action.** Specifically, the flow $\dot{y} = Ay - \langle Z, y\rangle y + \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + |y|^2)Z$ on $B^d$, when restricted to the boundary $|y| = 1$, recovers exactly the Kuramoto sphere equation.
3. **For identical oscillators** ($A_i = A$ for all $i$), the $N$-body flow is confined to a single group orbit $Gx_0$ of an initial configuration, and that orbit is a submanifold of dimension at most $d(d+1)/2$ — independent of $N$. The dynamics reduce to the group manifold itself.
4. Any point $x_i(t)$ on a trajectory can be written $x_i(t) = g_t(p_i)$ for some time-dependent Möbius transformation $g_t \in G$ and a fixed base configuration $p_i$, and $g_t$ satisfies an ODE on $G$ with coefficients determined by $A$ and $Z$ evaluated along the trajectory.

Writing $g_t(y) = \zeta_t M_{-z_t}(y)$ with $z_t \in B^d$ and $\zeta_t \in SO(d)$, the explicit reduced equations are

$$
\dot{z} \;=\; Az + \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + |z|^2)Z - \langle Z, z\rangle z, \qquad \dot{\zeta} \;=\; (A + \alpha(z, Z))\zeta,
$$

where $\alpha(z, Z)$ is a specific antisymmetric operator determined by the configuration. The first equation is a closed ODE on $B^d$ (once $Z$ and $A$ are specified as functions of $z$), and the second is a driven flow on $SO(d)$ slaved to the first. See [[ott-antonsen-ansatz]] for the framework.

## The continuum limit

In the thermodynamic limit $N \to \infty$, a configuration becomes a probability measure $\rho$ on $S^{d-1}$. The evolution is governed by the noiseless continuity equation associated with the flow:

$$\partial_t \rho + \nabla_{S^{d-1}}\cdot(\rho\, v) \;=\; 0, \qquad v(x) = Ax + Z - \langle Z, x\rangle x.$$

The Möbius group $G$ acts on measures by pushforward: $M_*\rho$. The orbit of the uniform measure $\sigma$ on $S^{d-1}$ under $G$ is the **Ott–Antonsen ansatz manifold** in this setting. Its elements are:

- **For $d = 2$**: Poisson kernel densities $\rho(\theta) = (2\pi)^{-1}(1 - |z|^2)/|e^{i\theta} - z|^2$ parameterised by $z \in \mathbb{D}$. This is the original Ott–Antonsen ansatz.
- **For $d \geq 3$**: **hyperbolic Poisson kernel** densities $\rho(x) = P_{\text{hyp}}(z, x)\, d\sigma(x)$ with $P_{\text{hyp}}(z, x) = [(1 - |z|^2)/|z - x|^2]^{d-1}$, parameterised by $z \in B^d$. These are the **higher-dimensional analogue** of the Ott–Antonsen ansatz, and they are solutions to the hyperbolic Dirichlet problem rather than the Euclidean one. The two families agree only at $d = 2$.

On the ansatz manifold, the continuum-limit dynamics reduce to the **same** ODE system as the finite-$N$ Möbius-group flow, parameterised by the Möbius element $z \in B^d$. This is what Lipton, Mirollo, and Strogatz call the "seamless connection between finite and infinite-$N$ dynamics" — the same group action controls both regimes.

## Gradient flow and global synchronization

For the special case of identical oscillators ($A_i = 0$) with a first-order linear order parameter $Z = \sum_i a_i x_i$ and positive weights $0 < a_i < 1/2$ summing to 1, the reduced $\dot{w}$ equation on $B^d$ is a **gradient flow** with respect to the hyperbolic metric. The explicit potential is

$$
\Phi(w) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{N} a_i \log\!\frac{1 - |w|^2}{|w - p_i|^2} \;=\; \frac{1}{d-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N} a_i\log P_{\text{hyp}}(w, p_i).
$$

The key properties:

1. $\Phi(w) \to -\infty$ as $|w| \to 1$ (Lemma 2 of the paper).
2. Every interior fixed point of the gradient flow in $B^d$ is *repelling* (Lemma 1).
3. $\Phi$ strictly decreases along trajectories.

**Theorem (Lipton–Mirollo–Strogatz §VIII).** For $N \geq 3$ and the assumptions above, almost all trajectories of the Kuramoto sphere system converge as $t \to \infty$ to the $(d - 1)$-dimensional diagonal manifold $\Delta \subset X$ — i.e., to the **completely phase-synchronized state** where all $x_i$ coincide. In backward time, the system is repelled from this state toward an incoherent configuration (codimension-$d$ subspace $\Sigma$ where $Z = 0$).

This is one of the few **global** synchronization theorems for any Kuramoto-type model, and it is a direct application of the hyperbolic gradient-flow construction. It illustrates the power of the Möbius-group framework as a potential alternative to the functional-analytic approach for Kuramoto stability questions.

## Relation to previous work

Several authors developed aspects of this reduction before Lipton–Mirollo–Strogatz unified them; [[lipton-mirollo-strogatz-2021-kuramoto-on-sphere]] §VII explicitly compares:

- **Lohe (2009, "Non-Abelian Kuramoto models and synchronization")** introduces the Kuramoto sphere model as one of two generalizations of Kuramoto to higher dimensions (the other being the matrix-valued Lohe model on $U(d)$, which this wiki has elected to scope out). Lohe derives a partial Möbius-group reduction but does not have a $\zeta$ equation analogous to Lipton's (5), and his order parameter $Z$ is independent of the reduced dynamics.
- **Tanaka (2014, "Solvable model for collective motion of heterogeneous particles")** derives a parallel reduction using Möbius transformations fixing the unit ball, with a different parameterisation. Writes the transformations differently from Lipton but uses the same group. Tanaka's continuum-limit equation is similar to Lipton's but expressed via $|z|^2$ terms that Lipton notes are puzzling and whose equivalence to his own form is not shown.
- **Chandra, Girvan, Ott (2019)** concentrates on the infinite-$N$ continuum limit and derives the dynamical reduction for a special class of probability densities on $S^{d-1}$ that generalize the Ott–Antonsen Poisson densities. Their densities are the hyperbolic Poisson kernels in disguise. Their main ODE agrees with Lipton's at the continuum-limit level, arrived at by a different route.

Lipton, Mirollo, and Strogatz's contribution is to **unify** these threads under a single group-theoretic framework spanning finite and infinite $N$, revealing the hyperbolic geometry of $B^d$ as the natural setting.

## Open questions (flagged in the paper)

§IX of [[lipton-mirollo-strogatz-2021-kuramoto-on-sphere]] lists directions for future work:

1. **Mixed-sign weights**: linear order parameters $Z = \sum a_i x_i$ where some $a_i$ can be negative. Could be explored via Lax-pair / Hamiltonian methods.
2. **Hamiltonian dynamics for even $d$**: for $d$ even, some systems admit Hamiltonian structure. Hamiltonian dynamics are only possible in even dimensions.
3. **Chimera states**: nonlinear order parameters may support chimera states (coexisting synchronized and desynchronized sub-populations) whose Möbius-group orbits are dynamically stable "without necessarily passing to the continuum limit" — an explicit hint that the finite-$N$ framework is powerful enough to handle chimeras.
4. **Multi-family networks**: Kuramoto-on-sphere networks with internal populations that differ in rotation $A_i$ or coupling weights across the sub-populations. The Möbius-group framework extends here without modification.

A standing theoretical question: whether the Möbius-group reduction can be extended to *non-identical* oscillators at non-zero frequency spread (where the group orbit picture breaks down). [[lipton-mirollo-strogatz-2021-kuramoto-on-sphere]] does not address this and neither do its antecedents; it is the central open question in the dimension-reduction line.
