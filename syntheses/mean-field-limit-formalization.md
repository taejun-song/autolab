# Mean-Field Limit Formalization Feasibility

Assessment of formalizing the propagation of chaos (N-particle to continuum PDE limit) for the Kuramoto model in Lean 4 / Mathlib.

## Mathematical Statement

**N-particle system**:
$$\dot\theta_i = \omega_i + \frac{K}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i), \quad i = 1,\ldots,N$$

**Continuum limit** (McKean-Vlasov / Vlasov equation):
$$\partial_t f + \partial_\theta\bigl[f \cdot v\bigr] = 0, \quad v(\theta,t) = \omega + K\int \sin(\theta'-\theta) f(\theta',t)\,d\theta'$$

**Mean-field limit theorem** (simplest Lipschitz version):

Let $F(\theta) = \sin(\theta)$ (Lipschitz with $\|F\|_{\mathrm{Lip}} = 1$). Suppose $(\theta_i(0))_{i=1}^N$ are i.i.d. with law $f_0$. Let $\mu_N(t) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \delta_{\theta_i(t)}$ be the empirical measure and $f(t)$ the solution of the continuum PDE with initial data $f_0$. Then for any $T > 0$:

$$\sup_{t \in [0,T]} W_1(\mu_N(t), f(t)) \xrightarrow{N\to\infty} 0 \quad \text{in probability}$$

where $W_1$ is the 1-Wasserstein distance.

**Quantitative version** (Dobrushin-type): If initial empirical measure satisfies $W_1(\mu_N(0), f_0) \le \varepsilon$, then
$$\sup_{t \in [0,T]} W_1(\mu_N(t), f(t)) \le \varepsilon \cdot e^{KT}$$

This deterministic Gronwall estimate is the core; the probabilistic convergence follows from concentration of empirical measures.

## Proof Strategy

The proof decomposes into three independent components:

### Step 1: Wasserstein stability of the continuum equation

If $f, g$ solve the continuum PDE with different initial data, then
$$W_1(f(t), g(t)) \le W_1(f(0), g(0)) \cdot e^{K \|F\|_{\mathrm{Lip}} \cdot t}$$

This follows from a Gronwall argument on the optimal coupling.

### Step 2: N-particle system as a self-consistent measure-valued ODE

The empirical measure $\mu_N(t)$ solves the continuum PDE exactly (it is a weak solution with atomic initial data). This is a structural observation: each particle evolves under the velocity field generated by $\mu_N$ itself.

### Step 3: Concentration of empirical measures

For i.i.d. samples from $f_0$ on $\mathbb{T}$ (compact), $\mathbb{E}[W_1(\mu_N, f_0)] \le C/\sqrt{N}$. Combined with Step 1, this gives the convergence rate.

### Simplest formalizable version

Drop the probabilistic layer entirely. Formalize only the **deterministic Gronwall estimate** (Step 1):

> For Lipschitz coupling $F$ with constant $L$, if two measure-valued solutions start at Wasserstein distance $\varepsilon$, they remain within $\varepsilon e^{KLt}$ for all time.

This is a clean functional-analytic statement with no probability theory required.

## Feasibility Assessment

### Tier 1 (Feasible now, 1-2 weeks): Deterministic Wasserstein contraction

Formalize: "If coupling $F$ is $L$-Lipschitz and two solutions of the particle ODE start $\varepsilon$-close in $\ell^1$, they remain $\varepsilon e^{KLt}$-close."

This avoids Wasserstein entirely and works at the finite-$N$ level. Uses only Gronwall + Lipschitz bounds already in Mathlib.

### Tier 2 (Moderate, 1-2 months): Wasserstein stability skeleton

Define $W_1$ for measures on $\mathbb{T}$ (the circle), prove it is a metric, prove the Gronwall stability estimate for the continuum equation. The circle case is simpler: $W_1$ on $\mathbb{T}$ has an explicit formula via distribution functions.

### Tier 3 (Hard, 3-6 months): Full mean-field limit

Combine Tiers 1-2 with concentration inequalities to get the probabilistic $N \to \infty$ limit. Requires building substantial probability infrastructure.

### Tier 4 (Research-level, 6-12 months): Quantitative rates with optimal constants

Match the $O(1/\sqrt{N})$ rate from Fournier-Guillin. This requires Kantorovich duality and optimal transport theory.

## Recommended Path

**Start with Tier 1**: the finite-$N$ Gronwall synchronization estimate. This:
- Uses only existing Mathlib infrastructure
- Produces a meaningful theorem (quantitative synchronization bound)
- Is a strict prerequisite for the full mean-field limit
- Connects directly to our existing `nPoleMeasure` and n-pole ODE work

**Key insight**: the sin coupling being 1-Lipschitz means the Kuramoto model is the cleanest possible test case for mean-field limits. No singular kernels, no blow-up, compact phase space.

## Connection to Existing Work

The mean-field limit provides the mathematical justification for why our continuum stability results (proved in `ContinuumDefinitive.lean`, `KuramotoEndToEnd.lean`) imply anything about finite-$N$ systems. Currently, this connection is an implicit assumption. Formalizing even Tier 1 would make this rigorous for the finite-$N$ case directly.
